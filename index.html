What is STEM Racing

A competition in which school teams work like a real racing team. They design and manufacture a miniature car powered by compressed air, race it down a 20-metre track, and find the partners to pay for it all.

Teams have three to six students aged 12 to 18. The track is only part of the score: judges also assess the engineering, the enterprise and marketing work, the pit display and a presentation by the team.

In Portugal it is run by the Technology Centres, as part of the Pense Indústria project. Around the world, the 2025 World Finals brought together 83 teams from 34 countries.

How a race works

  1. LaunchThe driver hits the trigger and compressed air fires the car. The gap between the signal and the press is the reaction time.
  2. Guide lineThe car runs along a line threaded through its eyelets, which keeps it in its lane.
  3. Finish gateAfter 20 metres the gate stops the clock.
  4. DecelerationThe car is caught at the end of the track. At the National Final the system uses the halo as the point of impact.

Each team races four times, twice in each lane. Race-time points use the average of the second and third best times. Reaction points use the best total time, reaction included.

Reaction

In STEM Racing the car is only half the race. Of the 220 race points, 110 are scored on a time that includes the driver's reaction.

Try it. The lights come on one by one. When they all go out, hit the button as fast as you can.

For scale: the fastest car at the 2025 World Finals covered the 20 metres in 1.118 s. A typical human reaction is about 0.25 s.

    Season 2025/26

    Our first season in STEM Racing Portugal, from naming the team to 8th place at the Regional Final.

    How it's scored

    In 2025/26 the winner was whoever scored the most out of 990 points. Racing was worth 220, less than a quarter.

    The rest was decided on engineering, enterprise, presentation and project management. Our regional final was held at CITEVE.

    What is STEM Racing

    • Race times, 110. Reaction races, 110.

    • Technical specifications, 120. Engineering drawings, 20. 3D rendering, 20. Quality of finish, 20.

    • Judged on the engineering portfolio: concepts, 3D modelling, simulation, manufacturing and testing.

    • Enterprise and marketing portfolio, 100. Pit display, 60. Team identity, 20. This is where we finished 4th.

    • Subject, 60. Technique, 40. Composition, 40. We finished 5th.

    • Initiating, 35. Planning, 25. Executing, 20. Monitoring and controlling, 10.

    The journey

    1. Identity

      Brand manual signed off in January 2026

      Aquila is Latin for “eagle” and the name of a constellation. The logo went to a vote: four proposals, six scores from 0 to 10, and the winner averaged 8.8.

      From that choice came the brand manual, the teamwear and everything that carries the team's name.

    2. Partners

      15 approaches, 10 agreements

      We contacted 15 companies and ten said yes, adding up to €4,908 in support. Nine of the ten backed us with services, materials or software rather than money.

      Meet the partners

    3. Car

      April 2026

      António and Tomás modelled the AR_01 in CAD and validated the design with CFD analysis. Bolflex 3D-printed components, Romafe supplied the bearings and Gigagraf the decals. We sanded and painted the body ourselves.

    4. Virtual pit display

      Built on Govie's platform

      A virtual pit display is usually a digital poster. Ours can be explored: three areas, for the team, the car and the partners, with videos and documents inside.

    5. Regional Final

      18 April 2026, CITEVE

      8th overall. 4th in the Enterprise Portfolio, 5th in the verbal presentation, 8th in engineering.
